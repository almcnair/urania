## First — what is Zodiacal Releasing?

Zodiacal Releasing (ZR) is an ancient Greek timing technique. It comes from a 2nd-century astrologer named **Vettius Valens**, in a text called the *Anthology* (Book IV). It was almost completely lost for centuries and was recovered and translated into modern practice in the last thirty years, largely through the work of Robert Schmidt (Project Hindsight) and Chris Brennan.

The idea is simple, even if the mechanics look complicated at first:

**Your life is divided into chapters. Each chapter has a ruler. The ruler tells you what the chapter is about.**

The chapters are called *periods*. They start when you're born and unfold in a fixed sequence for the rest of your life. Some are long (25 years), some are very short (a few days). They nest inside each other like Russian dolls — a big chapter contains medium chapters contains small chapters contains very small chapters. Astrologers call these **Level 1 (L1), Level 2 (L2), Level 3 (L3), and Level 4 (L4)**.

- **L1** is the biggest chapter you're in — think of it as the *era* of your life.
- **L2** is the sub-chapter within that era — think *year-scale*, sometimes 2–3 years.
- **L3** is a shorter movement within the L2 — think *month-scale*.
- **L4** is very short — a *few-days-scale* mood.

Each period is ruled by a sign of the zodiac and, therefore, by that sign's planet. So if the L2 period you're in is *Aquarius*, then Saturn (Aquarius's ruler) is running that sub-chapter of your life. Whatever Saturn means in your natal chart — where he sits, what he's connected to, whether he's dignified or afflicted — becomes the flavor of that period.

**Two starting points.** You can release from either of two calculated points in the chart:

- **Lot of Spirit** — traditionally read as your *career, direction of action, the arc of the soul's work in the world.* This is the one we're using for you.
- **Lot of Fortune** — traditionally read as your *body, health, livelihood, material life, the arc of your circumstances.*

Different questions, different starting Lot. For "what's happening in the trajectory of my work and calling," we release from Spirit. That's what I've been referring to in previous readings.

**Sources for what I just said:** Valens, *Anthology* IV.1–13 (the original); Chris Brennan, *Hellenistic Astrology* (2017), ch. 17 (the best modern treatment); Demetra George, *Ancient Astrology in Theory and Practice*, Vol. II (forthcoming/partial), also good.

## Two concepts you need before the reading

Two ideas do most of the heavy lifting. Once you have these two, the rest reads itself.

### 1. Peak periods

Each of your periods is ruled by a sign. When that sign happens to fall in an *angular* house *counted from your Lot of Spirit* — that is, the 1st, 4th, 7th, or 10th sign from Spirit — the period is called a **peak period**.

Peaks are exactly what they sound like: the periods where the trajectory *rises to a summit*. Career-visible moments, career-defining moments, moments where public reputation or vocation is loud. Not always good (a peak can be a peak of crisis), but always *significant*.

Your Lot of Spirit is at **17° Taurus 32'**, in your natal 11th house. Counting from Taurus:

- **1st sign from Spirit** = Taurus (peak — ruled by Venus)
- **4th sign from Spirit** = Leo (peak — ruled by the Sun)
- **7th sign from Spirit** = Scorpio (peak — ruled by Mars)
- **10th sign from Spirit** = Aquarius (peak — ruled by Saturn)

Any period ruled by one of these four signs is a peak. Every other period (Gemini, Cancer, Virgo, Libra, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Pisces, Aries) is *non-peak* — either background weather, buildup, or aftermath.

### 2. Loosing of the Bond

Sometimes the natural sequence of periods "jumps" — the release breaks its pattern and moves to a completely different sign, usually the sign *opposite* where it would have gone. This is called a **Loosing of the Bond** (LB — Valens's phrase, translated). It's a technical marker for *the trajectory pivots here, the previous arc completes, a new arc opens.*

LBs are structurally important. In Valens they're marked as points of *dramatic change* — a person's life-story literally taking a turn. Modern practitioners (Brennan especially) treat them as legitimate structural inflection points but caution against reading them as always dramatic-in-a-cinematic sense. Sometimes it's a quiet turn. But it is a turn.
