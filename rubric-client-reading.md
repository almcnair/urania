# Rubric — Client / Third-Party Readings

The measure I apply to any reading Austin asks me to write *for someone else* — a friend, a family member, a client he is coaching or advising. My own reads for Austin are governed by SOUL.md and the three-part shape; this rubric is stricter, because the subject is not developing astrological literacy and will not push back if a paragraph is padded.

The anchor example of **Distinguished** is `examples/donnie-eclipse-nationals-2026-08-12.md` — the eclipse-day reading Austin wrote for Donnie at USA Archery Nationals. When in doubt about a scoring call, compare against that reading directly.

---

## The Six Dimensions

### 1. Frame Economy — how much technique does the reading load-bear on?

*How many technical anchors does the reading declare, and does it use every one of them?*

- **Distinguished.** Two, at most three, technical anchors are named up front — and every one of them shows up again in the body. Nothing declared that isn't used. (Donnie: eclipse-to-Sun axis, 7th-house profection year, Mars as LoY. Three anchors, all three drive the week.)
- **Proficient.** Three to four anchors declared, most used. One or two mentioned once and dropped.
- **Developing.** Full dignity/sect/aversion table dumped up top. Reader is asked to hold framework they will not use.
- **Beginning.** No frame at all — just a list of transits.

### 2. Translation — is the technique in the reader's language, or in mine?

*Every naked term the subject cannot act on is a failure.*

- **Distinguished.** No unexplained jargon anywhere. Houses named by their felt-domain in the same sentence they appear ("1-on-1 relationships, contracts, and direct opponents" for the 7th). Traditional terms only when they earn it, and defined once when they do. The reader can act on every line without a glossary.
- **Proficient.** One or two terms defined on first use; the rest translated. No astrologer-shorthand.
- **Developing.** "Axis," "aversion," "peregrine," "dispositor" appear without explanation. Reader has to trust the astrologer that the words matter.
- **Beginning.** Cookbook language ("Mars in the 7th means…") with no chart-specific translation.

### 3. Transit-Line Discipline — does every named transit have event, time, and function in one breath?

*The Donnie-reading test: can I read one bullet aloud and the subject knows what it is, when it is, and what to do with it?*

- **Distinguished.** Every transit is one line, one clock time, one plain-English function. "Thu Aug 13 (5:12 PM): Mars square natal Pluto tests your composure and focus under pressure." Timing → event → so-that-means, tight.
- **Proficient.** Time and event given cleanly; the so-that-means takes a second sentence or drifts modal-heavy. Still actionable.
- **Developing.** Transits listed with degrees and orbs but no functional read. Reader is left to interpret.
- **Beginning.** Transit dump with no timing, or timing without event, or event without translation.

### 4. Synthesis Rhythm — does the week (or day, or reading) have a shape the subject can hold?

*The reader will remember a rhythm; they will not remember a table.*

- **Distinguished.** A single short rhythm at the end that the subject could recite from memory. Five days, five verbs. ("Tuesday settles you. Wednesday resets your center. Thursday tests your composure. Friday requires physical maintenance. Saturday rewards your past preparation.") The rhythm is the reading's takeaway; the body of the reading justifies it.
- **Proficient.** A synthesis paragraph that names the arc. Not memorizable, but coherent.
- **Developing.** A summary that repeats the transit list without shape.
- **Beginning.** No synthesis. The reading ends where the last transit ends.

### 5. Scope & Modal Discipline — does the reading know what astrology can and cannot do?

*One short paragraph, unpatronizing, ideally near the end.*

- **Distinguished.** A single paragraph that names the boundary and hands the tool back to the subject. No apology, no lecture. Every transit above stayed modal ("inclines," "favors," "tests") without a single indicative prediction. No medical, relationship-outcome, or fatalist language.
- **Proficient.** Modal register held throughout; boundary named but longer than it needs to be.
- **Developing.** One or two lines slip into indicative ("you will feel," "this will bring"). Scope paragraph missing or generic.
- **Beginning.** Predictions in the indicative. Outcomes named. Medical or relationship-outcome claims.

### 6. Filter — is anything in the reading that should not be there?

*Every sentence must earn its place. Background weather is the enemy.*

- **Distinguished.** Nothing extra. Outers named only when they perfect. Dignities named only when they change the read. No fixed-star sidebar, no zodiacal releasing footnote, no "also worth mentioning." If a placement is background weather, it is not in the reading.
- **Proficient.** One or two lines could be trimmed; overall the filter held.
- **Developing.** Several passages read as "showing my work." The subject has to skim past technique that is not for them.
- **Beginning.** The reading is a chart tour. Everything the astrologer knows is in it.

## The Composite Reading

A reading scores at the level of its **lowest** dimension. A Distinguished frame with a Beginning transit-line discipline is a Developing reading — because the reader will feel the lines that failed, not the frame that worked.

A reading I hand Austin for a friend or client should score **Proficient in every dimension and Distinguished in at least three** before I consider it finished. Anything below Proficient in any dimension is a draft, not a delivery.

---

## Length Discipline (a corollary)

The Donnie reading is roughly 500 words on the page. That is not a floor or a ceiling — but it is a useful reference. Longer readings are earned by *depth of technique legitimately in play* (a natal delineation with multiple timelord layers, a solar return covering a full year), not by *showing more of what I know* (adding placements the subject does not need to hear about).

Every additional 200 words needs to justify itself against Dimension 6 (Filter). If I cannot say what those words are doing for the subject, they are noise.

## Delivery — a note added 2026-08-08 against Donnie's website version

The rubric above judges the *reading*. When a reading is delivered as a website, deck, or other structured medium, two additional considerations apply — treated as modifiers on the six dimensions, not new dimensions.

**Two-layer delivery is legitimate.** A primary reader-facing surface (narrative, translated, no orbs) with a separate opt-in technical surface (positions, orbs, sources, method notes) is a strong way to satisfy Dimension 6 (Filter) without losing the arc of rigor that Dimension 1 (Frame Economy) implies. Donnie's site does this: the front page is the reading; `/the-astrology-behind-it` is the ephemeris backing. The technical page is exemplary in its own register (arcsecond precision, full meet-week perfections table, sources named) but is not shown to the subject unless they ask.

**Interactive containers enforce or violate transit-line discipline.** A day-card, expander, or accordion is a natural container for the one-line-per-transit format from Dimension 3. If each container holds *timing + event + function* in a compact form, the interaction reinforces the discipline. If the container hides the clock time or the function-read behind a click and the reader has to hunt, the interaction violates it. The rubric bar is the same either way — the medium does not lower it.

**The `not something wrong with me` move.** Donnie's site adds one line the source did not: *"When you feel a shift on Wednesday, recognize it as the eclipse resetting your center — not something wrong with me."* This is preemptive translation of a psychological reflex the subject will have. Worth naming as a technique: when a reading names a felt-state, name the misattribution the reader will make and defuse it in the same sentence. This is what Distinguished translation looks like at its best.

## What This Rubric Does Not Cover

- Austin's own daily and weekly briefings — those are governed by the three-part shape in SOUL.md and the transit-filter criteria in MEMORY.md. This rubric is stricter because Austin is a witch developing astrological literacy; a client or friend is not.
- Ritual electional work — a separate discipline. The rubric here is for interpretive readings, not electional windows.
- Group / party readings — I do not do them (SOUL.md).
